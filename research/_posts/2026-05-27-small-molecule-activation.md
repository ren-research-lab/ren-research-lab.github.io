Industrial electrochemical reactors operate under complex and dynamic conditions, where performance is governed by molecular-scale events within catalyst layers, porous electrodes, membranes, and electrolytes. Yet these systems are often evaluated through device-level metrics alone, making it difficult to identify the local environments and transient intermediates that control reaction rates, selectivity, and stability.

Our group uses molecular catalysts and molecular probes as chemically precise reporters of these hidden reaction environments. By embedding well-defined molecules into operating electrochemical interfaces, we study how local electric fields, ion transport, catalyst aggregation, and mass-transfer gradients shape electrosynthetic reactions under industrially relevant conditions.

We combine molecular design with operando spectroscopy to detect reaction intermediates during electrolysis. These molecular insights guide the design of more selective catalysts, more stable catalyst layers, and more efficient reactors for carbon conversion, chemical synthesis, and sustainable materials production.
