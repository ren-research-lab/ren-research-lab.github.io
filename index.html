01 / Lab overview

We develop electrochemical routes to recover, refine, and transform resources—from natural ores and minerals to industrial wastes and end-of-life materials.

Natural resources + Waste-derived resources

MERP combines electrochemistry, extractive metallurgy, materials chemistry, and reactor engineering to create lower-carbon pathways for producing the materials and chemicals society depends on.

Our approach

02 / Across scales

We use electricity as a precise tool for moving elements, controlling interfaces, and redesigning resource-intensive processes.

Our work connects molecular-level mechanisms with materials, electrochemical reactors, and process systems. This allows us to ask both fundamental questions—how ions, electrons, and molecules behave at electrified interfaces—and practical ones about selectivity, energy use, scale-up, and circularity.

The goal is to make resource processing more selective and less carbon-intensive, while creating new value from feedstocks that are currently discarded or underused.

Where we work

03 / Research
01

Critical mineral recovery

Selective electrochemical extraction, separation, and regeneration of valuable elements from ores, concentrates, residues, and end-of-life materials.

02

Waste valorization and circular processing

Electrified pathways that convert industrial wastes, mining residues, and manufacturing scrap into useful materials and chemical feedstocks.

03

Electrosynthesis and reactor engineering

Molecular probes, operando analysis, and scalable reactor design for controlling industrial electrochemical transformations.
